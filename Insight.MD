# Key Insights

*Computed directly from this project's dataset: a 10,000-order sample (Jan 2017 – Aug 2018) mapped from the real, public **Olist Brazilian E-Commerce dataset** into this project's schema — see [`map_olist_data.py`](map_olist_data.py). Currency is BRL (Brazilian Real), matching the source data.*

1. **Revenue grew steadily, ~16% average month-over-month**, climbing from ~R$14K (Jan 2017) to a peak of ~R$112K (Apr 2018) — consistent with Olist's real, well-documented growth phase as a marketplace during this period.
2. **Health & Beauty, Watches & Gifts, and Bed/Bath/Table are the top 3 categories**, together contributing ~26% of revenue — a much flatter distribution than a typical synthetic dataset, i.e., no single category dominates, which is realistic for a multi-category marketplace.
3. **The repeat purchase rate is only ~0.3%** (34 of 9,900 customers placed more than one order in this sample). This is a well-known, genuine characteristic of the Olist dataset — most sellers on the platform never see a returning buyer within the observed window. **This is worth explicitly calling out in an interview**: it means classic loyalty-focused RFM segmentation has limited signal on *this* dataset, and the more actionable question becomes acquisition-funnel and first-purchase-experience analysis rather than retention.
4. **The top 10% of customers by spend still account for ~42% of revenue**, even with such a low repeat rate — driven by order value variance (some customers place large multi-item orders), not repeat frequency. Good example of "value concentration without loyalty."
5. **97% of orders were delivered successfully**; combined cancel/unavailable/stuck-in-process orders were under 3% — a healthy fulfillment pipeline.
6. **Credit Card dominates payments (75.6% of orders)**, followed by Boleto (Brazil's bank-slip payment method, ~19.8%) — a market-specific pattern worth noting if asked about the data's geographic origin.
7. **Average review score is 4.1/5**, but the distribution is bimodal — 58% give 5 stars while 11.5% give 1 star, with very few 2-3 star (lukewarm) reviews. **Fixed Telephony** and **Construction Tools** categories rate notably lower (~3.1–3.7) than the platform average.
8. The "90-day churn" query in `analysis_queries.sql` measures inactivity relative to the *most recent order in this historical sample*, not real-world "today." Because the repeat-purchase rate is so low, ~82% of customers appear "churned" by that definition — but that's mostly a byproduct of one-time-buyer behavior, not customers actively lapsing. In a live production system, you'd instead measure recency relative to the current date. This is exactly the kind of nuance that's good to preempt in an interview.
